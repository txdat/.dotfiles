# /review-feature — Review a Plan

Read [plan.md](plan.md), [design-feature.md](design-feature.md), and [independence.md](independence.md). Design owns the ambiguity gate, AC/TC/fixture schema, scope and evidence criteria, and slicing rules; this review verifies them independently. Entry is `planning`. Unresolved ambiguity that materially affects in-scope correctness or the chosen design blocks readiness, including in an existing plan that claims to be ready.

## Review in order

Review scope separation before work slicing, applying [design-feature.md — Split scope before splitting work](design-feature.md#split-scope-before-splitting-work):

- Require separate API-contract, BE, and FE sub-plans for affected scopes. A combined plan with three sections or PR slices fails this boundary; unchanged or unaffected scopes need no empty plan.
- Review the API contract first, then review BE and FE against its exact reviewed revision. Verify that API semantics and shared fixtures have one authoritative source, references resolve, and consumers neither contradict nor silently extend the contract. Recheck both consumers when the contract changes.
- Check the overall outcome against the union of scoped Goals and ACs. Find missing responsibilities, conflicting ownership, circular prerequisites, and unowned integration verification. Integration TCs must name an owner, prerequisite state, and concrete expected results; passing against mocks alone cannot establish cross-scope correctness.
- Verify each sub-plan's work breakdown and PR Pattern independently using the delivery checks below. Distinguish plan prerequisites from branch parents and development dependencies from merge/release dependencies. Scope separation does not require one PR per scope or a forced BE-to-FE branch chain.

Report verdicts by exact plan path, with a separate coherence conclusion for the available set. A contract can be READY before consumer plans exist; that does not establish overall readiness. Missing required consumer plans or integration ownership blocks readiness of the full set, not an otherwise complete prerequisite contract. Apply the schema and verification requirements to each deliverable's scope, respecting design's documentation-only route.

1. **Derive independently.** Read the Goal, request, and source contracts before the proposed decisions, ACs, and TCs. Identify required outcomes, prohibited outcomes, failure conditions, and ambiguous terms from that evidence.
2. **Verify resolution and ACs.** Check the plan against the ambiguity gate: why each factor exists, what it resolves, how it is obtained or derived, and the evidence or authority for its resolution. A missing Open Questions section is not proof of resolution. Confirm that resolved behavioral semantics appear in the ACs and every required outcome is represented with decidable Success/Failure. Could all ACs pass while the Goal fails, or reject valid behavior? Report missing, unsupported, or conflicting obligations, including mechanism constraints without a requirement source. For unresolved interpretations, show a concrete scenario and differing consequences; route the finding through the ambiguity gate rather than inventing a user decision.
3. **Verify fixtures and coverage.** Apply [design-feature.md — Scope and evidence](design-feature.md#scope-and-evidence) to existing and proposed ACs/TCs. Trace required behavior to sufficient scenarios; flag unsupported obligations, unreachable fixtures, and redundant cases for removal or consolidation. Check `Proves:` against the scenario's actual purpose and verify each fixture reference and override resolves to sufficient inputs and starting state. Independently derive the expected result from the contract, including any permitted variability. Missing fixture data, an unspecified action, or an undecidable expected result for a required scenario blocks readiness. Challenge fixtures with plausible incorrect behavior grounded in the supported workflow; inspect combinations only where the interaction could change required behavior or create a distinct failure risk. Existing coverage that distinguishes the failure needs no additional TC.
4. **Inspect delivery.** Check TC-to-step mapping, [dependent impacts](../../CODING.md#impact), mechanism invariants, and non-functional commitments against the scope and project conventions. Check the proposed PR Pattern against design's slicing rules: each slice has a focused review purpose, includes its tests and implementation, and is designed to pass and merge after its parent without later work making it correct. Check intermediate compatibility, exposure of incomplete behavior, and reverts in reverse dependency order. For a large single PR, assess the stated coupling and identify a safe split if one exists; do not force a split by file count or architectural layer. This is a review of the planned boundaries; executable verification belongs to code review.
5. **Check complexity independently.** Apply [design-feature.md — Complexity and simpler alternatives](design-feature.md#complexity-and-simpler-alternatives) according to impact, regardless of plan type. Verify the impact assessment; accept a supported one-sentence no-impact explanation. For affected computational/resource costs, independently derive the typical/worst-case estimates and check workload evidence, conditional assumptions, allocation scope, and material I/O costs. Distinguish analytical bounds from measurements; accept justified measurement gaps with verification TCs under Open Risks rather than requiring benchmarks for every plan. Assess implementation complexity separately where affected. Warn about disproportionate cost or complexity and recommend a simpler viable alternative with relevant tradeoffs, or explain why the complexity is necessary. Missing required reasoning, unsupported feasibility claims, or demonstrated violations of required limits block readiness; optional simplifications do not.
6. **Reconcile prior findings.** After forming your own judgment, read recorded challenges and review history. Verify claimed corrections and whether recorded safeguards actually defeat the incorrect behavior; a prior verdict is not evidence that a gap is closed.

For a proposed missing scenario, identify the Goal or contract obligation, evidence that the scenario is reachable or explicitly required, and the concrete violation existing TCs would miss. Supply a distinguishing fixture and expected result. For an incomplete or ambiguous existing TC, identify the missing fact and why it prevents deciding correctness; do not invent the missing semantics. Possibility alone is not a finding. Optional hardening stays non-blocking, and redundant TCs alone call for consolidation rather than a blocked verdict. Stop expanding coverage when required behavior and supported material risks are covered. Review TC intent, fixture sufficiency, and expected-result correctness here; [review-code.md](review-code.md) verifies executable arrangements, production entry points, and assertions.

The counterexample in [design-feature.md's refund case](design-feature.md#example-partial-refunds) supports one obligation's coverage, not a READY verdict for the whole plan.

Report `READY` only when the ambiguity gate is satisfied, the Goal is covered, every TC has a sufficient concrete fixture and correct expected result, and required scenarios and delivery obligations are accounted for; otherwise `NEEDS CHANGES`. Give located findings, consequences, and required corrections. Optional preferences do not block. During delivery, the main agent records the verdict/evidence in `## Review History`; READY hands off to [approval.md](approval.md), never directly to implementation.
