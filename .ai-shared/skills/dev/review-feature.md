# /review-feature — Review a Plan

Read [plan.md](plan.md), [design-feature.md](design-feature.md), and [independence.md](independence.md). Design owns the ambiguity gate, AC/TC/fixture schema, scope and evidence criteria, and slicing rules; this review verifies them independently. Entry and exit remain `planning`; [approval.md](approval.md) owns the later transition to `approved`. Unresolved ambiguity that materially affects in-scope correctness or the chosen design blocks readiness, including in an existing plan that claims to be ready.

## Review in order

Review scope separation before work slicing, applying [design-feature.md — Split scope before splitting work](design-feature.md#split-scope-before-splitting-work):

- Verify the required scope sub-plans exist under that rule; sections or PR slices do not replace separate plans.
- Review the API contract first, then review BE and FE against its exact reviewed revision. Verify that API semantics and shared fixtures have one authoritative source, references resolve, and consumers neither contradict nor silently extend the contract. Recheck both consumers when the contract changes.
- Check the overall outcome against the union of scoped Goals and ACs. Find missing responsibilities, conflicting ownership, circular prerequisites, and unowned integration verification. Verify integration TCs meet the owning rule; passing against mocks alone cannot establish cross-scope correctness.
- Verify each sub-plan's work breakdown and PR Pattern independently using the delivery checks below. Distinguish plan prerequisites from branch parents and development dependencies from merge/release dependencies.

Report verdicts by exact plan path, with a separate coherence conclusion for the available set. A contract can be READY before consumer plans exist; that does not establish overall readiness. Missing required consumer plans or integration ownership blocks readiness of the full set, not an otherwise complete prerequisite contract. Apply the schema and verification requirements to each deliverable's scope, respecting design's documentation-only route.

1. **Derive independently.** Read the Goal, request, and source contracts before the proposed decisions, ACs, and TCs. Identify required outcomes, prohibited outcomes, failure conditions, and ambiguous terms from that evidence.
2. **Verify resolution and ACs.** Independently derive the required outcomes and their Success/Failure conditions from the Goal and source contracts, then compare them with the proposed ACs under the [ambiguity gate](design-feature.md#resolve-ambiguity-before-writing) and [AC derivation rules](design-feature.md#derivation). A missing Open Questions section is not proof of resolution. Could all ACs pass while the Goal fails, or reject valid behavior? Report missing, unsupported, or conflicting obligations, including mechanism constraints without a requirement source. For unresolved interpretations, show a concrete scenario and differing consequences; return to the ambiguity gate rather than inventing a user decision.
3. **Verify fixtures and coverage.** Apply the [schema](design-feature.md#plan-schema) and [scope and evidence criteria](design-feature.md#scope-and-evidence) to existing and proposed ACs/TCs. Trace required behavior to sufficient scenarios; flag unsupported obligations, unreachable fixtures, and redundant cases. Check `Proves:` against actual purpose and resolve each fixture reference and override. Independently derive expected results from the contract, including permitted variability, and challenge fixtures with plausible incorrect behavior. Missing fixture data, an unspecified action, or an undecidable expected result for a required scenario blocks readiness. Apply design's interaction and adequacy criteria before requesting additional TCs.
4. **Inspect delivery.** Independently derive dependency order and safe delivery boundaries from affected contracts and invariants, then compare them with the proposed steps and PR slices. Check TC-to-step mapping, [dependent impacts](../../CODING.md#impact), mechanism invariants, and non-functional commitments against the scope and project conventions. Apply [PR slicing](design-feature.md#pr-slicing) to each proposed boundary, including intermediate compatibility, exposure of incomplete behavior, and revert safety. Challenge a large single PR's coupling and identify a safe split if one exists. Review the planned boundaries here; executable verification belongs to code review.
5. **Check complexity independently.** Apply [Complexity and simpler alternatives](design-feature.md#complexity-and-simpler-alternatives) according to impact, regardless of plan type. Verify the impact assessment; accept a supported one-sentence no-impact explanation. Where costs change, independently derive typical/worst-case estimates and verify the owning rule's assumptions and evidence requirements, distinguishing analytical bounds from measurements. Assess implementation complexity separately; evaluate a simpler viable alternative or justify the selected complexity. Missing required reasoning, unsupported feasibility claims, or demonstrated violations of required limits block readiness; justified measurement gaps with verification TCs and optional simplifications do not.
6. **Reconcile prior findings.** After forming your own judgment, read recorded challenges and review history. Verify claimed corrections and whether recorded safeguards actually defeat the incorrect behavior; a prior verdict is not evidence that a gap is closed.

For a proposed missing scenario, identify the Goal or contract obligation, evidence that the scenario is reachable or explicitly required, and the concrete violation existing TCs would miss. Supply a distinguishing fixture and expected result. For an incomplete or ambiguous existing TC, identify the missing fact and why it prevents deciding correctness; do not invent the missing semantics. Possibility alone is not a finding. Optional hardening stays non-blocking, and redundant TCs alone call for consolidation rather than a blocked verdict. Stop expanding coverage when required behavior and supported material risks are covered. Review TC intent, fixture sufficiency, and expected-result correctness here; [review-code.md](review-code.md) verifies executable arrangements, production entry points, and assertions.

The [refund example](reference/design-feature-examples.md#example-partial-refunds) illustrates coverage of balance behavior; its counterexample does not establish readiness of a complete refund plan.

Report `READY` only when the ambiguity gate is satisfied, the Goal is covered, every TC has a sufficient concrete fixture and correct expected result, and required scenarios and delivery obligations are accounted for; otherwise `NEEDS CHANGES`. Give located findings, consequences, and required corrections. Optional preferences do not block. During delivery, the main agent records the verdict/evidence in `## Review History`; READY hands off to [approval.md](approval.md), never directly to implementation.
