# IELTS Assessment — Evidence Before Bands

This file owns Writing/Speaking descriptors and assessment evidence limits. [README.md](README.md) owns shared scoring and provenance.

## Authoritative sources

Checked 2026-09-05:

- [Writing band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf), updated May 2023: use the Academic Task 1 or Task 2 criteria.
- [Speaking band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf): use the Speaking tables, including their separate LR and GRA descriptors.

Use the relevant official table or a verified supplied copy when accessible; reuse it within the session. When unavailable, use the local anchors below for provisional estimates within bands 5–8. Record `rubric_basis: local-summary`, cite this file, and state the adjacent-band uncertainty. PDF access is not a prerequisite for those coaching estimates. With the full table, record `rubric_basis: official-descriptors` and its reference.

These abbreviated, paraphrased anchors omit finer distinctions. Compare neighboring rows for each criterion, then cite the response pattern supporting the choice and its limit. Do not convert isolated keywords or error counts into scores. If evidence falls outside these anchors, obtain the full scale or leave that criterion `null` with a qualitative explanation; never force a 5 or 8 boundary score.

## Offline Writing anchors

TA applies only to Academic Task 1; TR only to Task 2. The other columns apply within either writing task. [Source: Writing descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf), Academic Task 1 and Task 2 tables.

| Band | TA | TR | CC | LR | GRA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Features inadequately covered; detail overwhelms overview | Incomplete coverage; weak development | Uneven organization; repetitive connections | Limited choices; errors sometimes obscure meaning | Repetitive structures; complex attempts often faulty |
| 6 | Relevant overview attempted; supporting detail uneven | Main demands covered; support uneven | Overall progression; mechanical connections | Adequate meaning; imprecision | Simple/complex mix; errors seldom obscure meaning |
| 7 | Clear overview, grouping and main comparisons | Developed position; support sometimes generalized | Logical progression; flexible connections | Some precision/flexibility; occasional inappropriate choices | Varied complex forms; many accurate sentences |
| 8 | Requirements sufficiently covered; well-illustrated features | Developed position; ideas well supported | Easily followed; well-managed cohesion | Broad, precise, flexible vocabulary | Flexible range; mostly accurate sentences; occasional slips |

## Offline Speaking anchors

Use only with the evidence allowed below. [Source: Speaking descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf), pages 1–2. Speaking LR/GRA assess spoken language, separately from Writing.

| Band | FC | LR | GRA | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Continued speech relies on slowing/repeating; complexity disrupts flow | Limited flexibility; paraphrases sometimes fail | Basic forms controlled; complex forms commonly faulty | Beyond limited sound/stress control; not yet consistently understandable with little effort |
| 6 | Sustains turns; hesitation can disrupt coherence | Sufficient range; paraphrase usually succeeds | Structural mix; complex errors rarely obscure meaning | Usually understandable; stress/rhythm control varies |
| 7 | Sustains coherent long turns despite language searches | Flexible across topics; effective paraphrase | Flexible structures; many accurate sentences | Meets 6; shows some broader, sustained control of 8 |
| 8 | Fluent development; pauses mainly concern content | Broad, precise, flexible use; effective paraphrase | Broad range; mostly accurate sentences | Easily understood; sustained rhythm and flexible stress/intonation |

## Writing evidence map

Assess each criterion against its own evidence:

| Criterion | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| TA — Academic Task 1 | Source visual/data, overview, feature selection, comparisons, accuracy |
| TR — Task 2 | Response to the actual question, position, relevant development and support |
| CC | Progression, paragraph organization, reference, connections between ideas |
| LR | Precision, appropriacy, range, word formation and spelling |
| GRA | Range and control of structures, grammar and punctuation |

Name the best-fitting descriptor, response evidence, and limiting feature for each criterion. Explain uncertainty between adjacent bands; half-band estimates are coaching judgments. Missing prompt/visual → no TA/TR score or task average; language feedback can continue.

Do not assign a fixed deduction for a single mistake, short length, or a missing prompt part. Apply the relevant descriptor to the whole response. Use `null` for insufficient evidence; band zero is not a missing-data marker.

## Speaking evidence map

Use spoken-language evidence for the Speaking criteria:

| Criterion | Evidence to inspect |
|---|---|
| FC | Sustained delivery, hesitation/self-correction, relevance and development |
| LR | Flexible vocabulary, meaning, idiomatic use and paraphrase |
| GRA | Spoken structures and grammatical control; not transcript punctuation |
| Pronunciation | Intelligibility, sounds, stress, rhythm and intonation heard in audio |

### Transcript-only assessment

- Confirm whether the text is verbatim, edited, or automatic transcription. Do not attribute possible transcription mistakes to the speaker without checking.
- A verbatim sample may support provisional LR/GRA estimates if long enough to show a pattern. A brief answer or edited script receives qualitative language feedback instead of a performance band.
- Discuss visible coherence and any explicitly preserved disfluencies. Leave the full FC score `null`; punctuation and polished wording do not reveal delivery.
- Pronunciation stays `null` without accessible audio. A phonetic note may identify something to check, not prove stress or linking quality.
- Overall/estimated Speaking band stays `null`. Do not average two or three available criteria into a substitute band.

When audio is available and the environment can actually inspect it, tie observations to timestamps and distinguish a short sample from evidence across the test. Do not claim to have heard inaccessible audio.
